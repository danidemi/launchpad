# Naming Approaches

Techniques to draw from when generating candidates. Use several, not just
one, so the shortlist isn't all the same shape.

- **Descriptive**: names that state what it does (e.g. "PriceCheck").
  Clear, low memorability, hard to trademark broadly.
- **Compound**: two real words joined (e.g. "Snapchat", "Dropbox"). Good
  balance of clarity and distinctiveness.
- **Metaphor**: borrows an unrelated concept that evokes the right feeling
  (e.g. "Amazon", "Oracle"). High distinctiveness, needs marketing to
  explain what it does.
- **Invented / coined**: a new word, often from altered real words or
  foreign-language roots (e.g. "Spotify", "Kodak"). Maximum trademark
  strength, needs the most spelling/pronunciation care.
- **Founder or place name**: (e.g. "Ferrari", "Tesla"). Works when there's
  a real story behind it; weak otherwise.
- **Acronym**: only recommend if the underlying phrase is genuinely used by
  the target customer already — otherwise it's meaningless letters.

## Red Flags to Screen Out

- Awkward when said aloud, or ambiguous spelling from speech alone.
- Negative or comedic meaning in Italian, English, or other markets the
  business targets.
- Too close to a dominant existing brand in the same or an adjacent
  category (confusion risk, not just competition).
- Locks the name to one product when the business may expand
  ("[City]Taxi" if ride types beyond taxi are planned).
